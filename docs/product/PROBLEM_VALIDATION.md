### Validated Problem Areas (Where SARA Adds Value)

Despite CPGRAMS's significant evolution, the following gaps remain well-documented:

#### 1. "Marked as Resolved" Without Genuine Resolution

- **Problem**: Grievances may be closed in the system without the citizen feeling their problem was genuinely solved. High disposal rates don't always equal citizen satisfaction.
- **Evidence**: 76% satisfaction rate means ~24% of citizens are dissatisfied with resolutions. The appeal mechanism exists but is reactive, not proactive.
- **SARA's answer**: Mandatory evidence-backed resolution verification with citizen confirmation BEFORE closure. AI-assisted evidence analysis to detect resolution claims lacking substance.

#### 2. "Pass-the-Buck" Syndrome

- **Problem**: Some departments dispose of grievances by suggesting the citizen approach another agency, or by routing the complaint back to the very office against which it was filed.
- **Evidence**: Documented in DARPG reviews and media reports.
- **SARA's answer**: Smart routing with AI classification confidence scoring, circular routing detection, and accountability tracking across transfers.

#### 3. Procedural Compliance vs. Genuine Outcome

- **Problem**: Departments may focus on meeting KPIs (closing within 21 days) rather than addressing root causes.
- **Evidence**: Documented tension between timeline compliance and outcome quality.
- **SARA's answer**: Outcome-focused governance engine that evaluates resolution quality, not just speed. Risk scoring that accounts for evidence quality, citizen confirmation, and pattern analysis.

#### 4. Administrative Inaction at Ground Level

- **Problem**: AI can classify and route, but it cannot force an unresponsive officer to act. The gap between "monitoring" a breach and "accountability" for that breach is significant.
- **Evidence**: Multiple sources identify this as the primary bottleneck.
- **SARA's answer**: Proactive risk prediction, automated reminders, configurable escalation policies, and structured accountability dossiers that give supervisors actionable intelligence.

#### 5. Lack of Justification for Closure

- **Problem**: Many departments fail to provide valid reasons for closing grievances, undermining transparency.
- **Evidence**: DARPG mandates but enforcement is inconsistent.
- **SARA's answer**: Mandatory evidence and justification workflow states. Resolution cannot transition to CLOSED without verification evidence and citizen confirmation.

#### 6. Algorithmic Accountability Gap

- **Problem**: As AI influences routing and prioritization, it's unclear who is responsible when AI misclassifies a serious issue. "Black-box" AI decisions resist auditing.
- **Evidence**: Documented concern in governance and AI ethics literature.
- **SARA's answer**: Explainable AI with confidence scores, classification reasoning, and complete audit trails. Every AI recommendation is logged with inputs, outputs, and confidence.

#### 7. Fragmented Cross-System Visibility

- **Problem**: Grievances may exist across CPGRAMS, state portals, department portals, and municipal systems with limited cross-visibility.
- **Evidence**: Despite API integration progress, real-time cross-system accountability monitoring is limited.
- **SARA's answer**: Interoperability adapter architecture with canonical grievance model. SARA can ingest from and push to multiple systems while maintaining a unified accountability view.

## Conclusion

SARA does not need to replace CPGRAMS. SARA addresses a specific, validated capability gap:

> **Proactive, explainable, case-level accountability intelligence with evidence-aware resolution verification and configurable governance policy enforcement.**

This is the defensible value proposition for SIH.
